    US work authorization and pay types

    The terms I explain most often on first calls.

    W2 (employee)

    You are an employee of the employer or staffing firm. Taxes are withheld from your pay, and benefits may be included depending on the company.

    C2C (corp-to-corp)

    You contract through your own company, which bills the vendor or client. You handle your own taxes, insurance and benefits.

    1099 (independent contractor)

    You are paid directly as an individual contractor with no tax withholding. You are responsible for your own taxes and insurance.

    H-1B

    A work visa for specialty occupations that requires an employer to sponsor and file the petition. If you already hold one, a new employer can file a transfer petition. Cap-subject new filings follow the annual registration process.

    OPT and STEM OPT

    F-1 students can work in their field after their program under OPT. Eligible STEM degrees can extend it with STEM OPT, which requires an employer enrolled in E-Verify and a training plan.

    US citizens, green card holders and EAD holders

    US citizens and permanent residents can work without employer sponsorship. EAD holders can work under the terms printed on their card.

    General information only, not legal advice. Check your situation with an immigration attorney.

    Common questions

    Do candidates pay a recruiter?

    No. Reputable recruiters are paid by the employer or staffing firm. If anyone asks you for money to place you, walk away.

    Will you submit my resume without asking?

    No. I confirm the role, company and your rate or salary with you before I submit you anywhere.

    What should I send first?

    Your updated resume, your target role, your location and work authorization, and your expected rate or salary.

    I am hiring. What do you need from me?

    The role title, must-have skills, location or remote policy, engagement type, budget range and the interview steps.
